SourcesBrain model: Shiu et al. (2024), MIT code; FlyWire v783 (CC BY-NC 4.0). Receptor responses: DoOR 2.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0). VNC: MaleCNS v1.0 (CC BY 4.0). The garden visualizes neural readouts, not simulated body physics.

    
Twelve frames a secondCharacters are redrawn every frame, the background every other frame. Each line has three versions that cycle, so outlines “boil” the way they do in hand-drawn animation.

    
6 wingbeats, not 200A real Drosophila is 2.5 mm long and beats its wings about 200 times a second. This one gets six; the smear lines fill in the rest.

    
Smells from DoOREach fruit selects one explicitly named chemical proxy from measured receptor-response data. It is a chosen input, not a reconstruction of the fruit’s full smell. The garden uses the pet’s current learned memory.

    
A sketch of neural signalsThe large fly’s reactions use simulated neural readouts; its flight path is an illustration, not simulated biomechanics. Day, night and the small background flies are scenery. Public visitors have separate temporary memories; the neural simulation is shared.
